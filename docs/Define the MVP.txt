Define the MVP

Our goal is not to replace a human receptionist.

Our goal is to ensure a business never misses a customer inquiry.

The promise is simple:

> "Every customer gets an immediate response, every lead is captured, and every appointment request is handled 24/7."




---

Target Market

Don't launch for everyone.

Pick one industry first.

I would choose one of these:

Barbershops

Beauty salons

Medical clinics

Insurance agencies

Funeral homes

Auto repair shops


Each has similar appointment and inquiry workflows.


---

Core Features (Version 1)

1. AI Chat

The AI answers questions like:

Opening hours

Prices

Services

Location

Parking

Payment methods

Staff

Policies



---

2. Lead Capture

Instead of ending with:

> "Thanks."



It asks:

Name

Phone

Email

Reason for visit


Every conversation becomes a lead.


---

3. Appointment Requests

The AI can:

Ask preferred date.

Ask preferred time.

Ask service.

Ask preferred staff member.


Initially, even if the booking still requires owner approval, that's enough to deliver value.


---

4. Owner Dashboard

The owner sees:

Conversations

Leads

Appointment requests

Customer history

AI performance



---

5. Business Knowledge

During setup, the owner fills out:

Business name

Hours

Address

Services

Prices

FAQ

Policies


This becomes the AI's knowledge base.

Technical Stack

You've already chosen a stack that fits well:

Frontend: Next.js + Tailwind + shadcn/ui

Backend: FastAPI

Execution Engine: PyRunner

Database: Supabase

AI: Multi-provider (OpenAI, Gemini, Ollama, etc.)

Deployment: Docker



---

Internal Architecture

Rather than a single chatbot, think in terms of services:

Website Chat
        │
        ▼
Conversation Service
        │
        ▼
Intent Router
        │
 ┌──────┼──────────┐
 ▼      ▼          ▼
FAQ   Booking   Lead Capture
        │
        ▼
Business Knowledge
        │
        ▼
Database

This modular design means new capabilities can be added without rewriting the whole system.

The One Rule

This is the principle I'd keep coming back to:

*Every feature must either help the business win more customers, save time, or reduce missed opportunities.



If a feature doesn't clearly do one of those things, it belongs later—not in the first release.
